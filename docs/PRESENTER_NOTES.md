## Slide 04: 01  /  CONTEXT

PQC refers to cryptographic algorithms meant for ordinary computing platforms. Unlike QKD, PQC does not rely on quantum transmission hardware. It is designed against known quantum algorithms; security depends on assumptions and correct deployment.

[Sources]
https://csrc.nist.gov/projects/post-quantum-cryptography

## Slide 05: 01  /  CONTEXT

AES is not asymmetric encryption. SHA-256 is a hash function, not encryption. TLS 1.3 uses ephemeral key agreement and authenticated encryption; older RSA key transport differs. Shor threatens factoring and discrete logarithms, while Grover offers ideal quadratic search speedups.

## Slide 07: 02  /  THREAT

RSA key generation chooses large primes. Factoring N undermines related secret information. ECC uses discrete logarithms in suitable elliptic-curve groups. Shor has polynomial-time algorithms for factoring and discrete logarithm on an ideal fault-tolerant quantum computer.

## Slide 09: 02  /  THREAT

For suitable a, find order r such that a^r=1 mod N. If r is even and a^(r/2) is not -1 modulo N, nontrivial gcd factors can be found. Ideal QFT probabilities concentrate near integer multiples of 2^t/r. Toy simulation explicitly prepares the modular oracle state and applies an inverse-QFT FFT.

## Slide 15: 03  /  MATHEMATICS

LWE: given A and b=A*s+e modulo q, recover s or distinguish b from random under specified distributions. Without noise, linear algebra can solve appropriately full-rank systems. ML-KEM uses module structure over polynomial rings, not this simplistic scalar equation as a complete specification.

[Sources]
https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/fips/203/final

## Slide 16: 03  /  MATHEMATICS

For ML-KEM, the ring R_q is a quotient polynomial ring and vectors/matrices live in module spaces. Security relies on Module-LWE related assumptions plus correct CCA-secure KEM design. Explain how errors frustrate direct linear-algebra recovery without falsely asserting every lattice problem resists all possible quantum attacks.

[Sources]
https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/fips/203/final

## Slide 17: 03  /  MATHEMATICS

Do not say PQC is proved unbreakable. Each primitive has security notions (KEM IND-CCA, EUF-CMA for signatures, etc.) and depends on concrete parameters. The point of migration is to move away from known polynomial-time quantum algorithms targeting factoring and discrete logarithms.

## Slide 19: 04  /  STANDARDS

This equation is an explanatory simplification of the underlying K-PKE component, not a verbatim full specification of FIPS 203. The public t=A*s+e contains noise. An encapsulating party constructs u=A^T*r+e1 and v=t^T*r+e2+Encode(m). The decapsulating party cancels s^T*u, leaving an encoded message plus a controlled small error. Correct parameter choices enable decoding. The actual ML-KEM uses polynomial modules, NTT operations, compression, hashing, and a CCA-secure transform; these must never be omitted from implementation.

[Sources]
https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/fips/203/final

## Slide 22: 04  /  STANDARDS

ML-DSA (FIPS 204), derived from CRYSTALS-Dilithium, signs messages using module-lattice-related mathematical assumptions. A signature does not encrypt message data. In source, sign and verification operate on a synthetic document; changing one byte makes verification fail.

[Sources]

## Slide 23: 04  /  STANDARDS

The Dilithium-style construction is not merely Sign(sk,m). A public key encodes t approximately A*s1+s2; a signer forms a masked commitment w=A*y, hashes a message-related commitment into challenge c and returns z=y+c*s1 plus hint material after rejection tests. A verifier reconstructs the challenge using public data and checks bounds. The slide suppresses NTT, rounding, t0/t1, context and rejection details and must not be used as implementation pseudocode.

[Sources]
https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/fips/204/final

## Slide 37: 06  /  IMPLEMENTATION

A practical PQC transition requires cryptographic inventory, dependency analysis, risk ranking, crypto agility, interoperability testing, certificate format and protocol support, hardware resources, rollback, implementation review and governance. U.S. NIST transition guidance can inform organizations globally, but legal deadlines depend on jurisdiction.
